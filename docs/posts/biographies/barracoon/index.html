Barracoon by Zora Neale Hurston

  • #Biographies

Zora Neale Hurston · 1 min read

Cudjo Lewis’s oral history of capture, the Clotilda crossing, enslavement, and life in Africatown

Kossola’s life in his own words

Zora Neale Hurston’s Barracoon: The Story of the Last “Black Cargo” preserves the testimony of Oluale Kossola, also known as Cudjo Lewis. Hurston first met him in 1927 and returned for interviews that became the basis of the book. She completed the manuscript in 1931; it was first published in 2018.

Kossola recalls his family and community in West Africa, the raid that led to his capture, confinement in a barracoon, and forced passage aboard the Clotilda. The ship reached Alabama illegally in 1860. His account continues through enslavement and freedom in 1865.

Making a home after displacement

After emancipation, Kossola and other survivors helped establish Africatown. His memories include family life, bereavement, and a continuing longing for the home from which he had been taken.

Hurston’s attention to his manner of speaking is central to the book. The narrative asks readers to hear a particular person, rather than reducing him to a historical statistic. Although once described as the last survivor, Kossola was outlived by other Clotilda survivors, including Redoshi and Matilda McCrear.

Sources: National Park Service and Smithsonian on the manuscript and Africatown.
