Photocopies by John Berger

  • #Biographies

John Berger · 1 min read

Short written portraits drawn from John Berger’s encounters with people and places

Lives caught in brief encounters

John Berger’s Photocopies is a collection of short written portraits. The collection approaches lives through brief encounters rather than a continuous life story. Berger builds the book from encounters with people in different places, letting particular gestures and situations carry the portrait.

The people include a shepherd, a farmer, artists, and people encountered while travelling. The settings range across cities and rural landscapes. Their variety matters: the book moves between lives without turning them into a single story of professional achievement.

Attention as a way of understanding

A useful way to read the collection is to notice how much a small observation can suggest about another person. Rather than supplying a complete life history, each piece offers a partial view. The gaps remain part of the encounter, inviting the reader to attend to the person without assuming that a brief description can explain everything about them.

The title suggests this process of preserving an impression. Berger’s medium here is prose, and the portraits depend on the act of looking, remembering, and choosing what to describe.
