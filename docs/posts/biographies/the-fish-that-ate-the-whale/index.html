The Fish That Ate the Whale by Rich Cohen

Sam Zemurray’s rise from immigrant fruit seller to United Fruit leader, and the costs of banana empire

Sam Zemurray and the banana business

Rich Cohen’s The Fish That Ate the Whale: The Life and Times of America’s Banana King tells the story of Sam Zemurray, an immigrant entrepreneur who rose from selling fruit to controlling United Fruit.

The biography follows a business built around a perishable product. Zemurray’s early work as a peddler and banana hauler gave him practical knowledge of the trade. He expanded into plantations and became a challenger to the much larger United Fruit Company before taking a leading role in it.

Enterprise and its costs

Cohen presents Zemurray’s determination alongside the political consequences of the industry. The banana business connected American markets to land, labour, and power in Central America. Commercial ambition could reach into the affairs of governments, making the story larger than one man’s rise to wealth.

The central tension is between opportunity and domination. Zemurray’s career illustrates the possibilities available to an ambitious newcomer, while also raising questions about corporate power and the treatment of other countries as instruments of profit. The book invites readers to consider both sides of that record.
