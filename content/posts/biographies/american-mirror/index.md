## The illustrator and the private man

Deborah Solomon's *American Mirror: The Life and Art of Norman Rockwell* examines the distance between Rockwell's reassuring public imagery and his unsettled private life.

Rockwell's work for *The Saturday Evening Post* turned ordinary encounters into carefully staged stories. Solomon considers the craft behind those pictures and the way they expressed an American ideal of belonging. The familiar scenes were constructed visions of community, rather than a complete record of American society.

## Work, anxiety, and belonging

Drawing on letters and other documents, Solomon connects Rockwell's working habits with his loneliness, depression, and doubts about his own ability. His professional success did not resolve the difficulties in his family life. The biography also follows his place in a changing visual culture as photography and television altered the role of illustration.

Rockwell's later engagement with civil rights complicates the idea that he painted only comforting nostalgia. Solomon treats his storytelling as a serious artistic achievement while asking what his images reveal, and what they leave outside the frame.
